# Week 12 outline: Mechanisms, Moderators, and Natural Experiments

**Working title:** "When you can't randomize: mechanisms, moderators, and natural experiments"

**Readings:** Craig & Richeson (2014; 2015 corrigendum), carried over from Week 11 · Mutz & Rao (2018), "The Real Reason Liberals Drink Lattes" · Petty, Cacioppo & Goldman (1981), "Personal Involvement as a Determinant of Argument-Based Persuasion" · Dunning (2012), *Natural Experiments in the Social Sciences*, ch. 1 · Grosz et al. (2024), "Natural experiments: Missed opportunities for causal inference in psychology" · Bol, de Vaan & van de Rijt (2018), "The Matthew effect in science funding" · Balcells & Torrats-Espinosa (2018), "Using a natural experiment to estimate the electoral consequences of terrorist attacks"

**Arc in one sentence:** Finish Week 11's Craig & Richeson case, which manipulated a proposed mechanism. Use it to revisit mediation (Mutz & Rao: measuring candidate mechanisms in observational data) and moderation (Petty, Cacioppo & Goldman: an effect that depends on involvement). Then ask what to do when we can't randomize at all: Dunning's and Grosz's natural-experiment toolbox, each design drawn as a naive DAG vs. a design DAG with its assumptions, and two full cases (Bol et al.'s funding threshold; Balcells & Torrats-Espinosa's attacks during survey fieldwork).
